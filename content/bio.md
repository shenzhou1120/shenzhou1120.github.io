My research aims to understand and model dynamic land-surface processes from heterogeneous Earth observation data. During my master’s study, my research focused on process-based crop growth modelling, which provided me with a physical understanding of soil–plant–atmosphere interactions and how land-surface states evolve in response to environmental drivers.

During my PhD, I shifted toward high-resolution crop yield prediction using Earth observation data. A central challenge was the scarcity of high-resolution crop yield labels: producing 10 m yield maps across Heilongjiang Province had to rely on only around 5,000 sparse and noisy field samples. This limitation led me to investigate how robust representations could be learned with much weaker supervision. I first explored multi-task learning, using related agricultural tasks to provide complementary supervision, and subsequently turned to self-supervised learning from large volumes of unlabeled remote sensing observations.

This progression gradually shifted my research focus from crop yield prediction itself to spatiotemporal representation learning for Earth observation. My current research focuses on developing dynamic representations of land-surface states from heterogeneous, asynchronous, and incomplete observations to support downstream agricultural applications.
